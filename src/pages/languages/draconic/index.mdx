<Drop>D</Drop>raconic is the language of the dragons in the Material Plane, originating around the Spring of the Earth. It comes in two main dialects: eastern and western, *rékharhákr* and *rkaarik*, with limited intelligibility between them. There is also the ancient literary form of the language “*hakr*”, which is only preserved for highly formal and religious occasions, and is generally not known by outsiders.

This language is explained using the eastern version of Draconic, which is spoken in East Aseron and the Spring of the Earth. Later sections show differences between this, the western dialect, and ancient draconic.

## Eastern Draconic : rekharhakr

> [!readaloud]
> <p class="centre"><Drac>REKHARHAKR</Drac></p>

### Introduction

*rekharhakr*, or eastern draconic, is the variant of draconic spoken around and east of the Spring of the Earth, in East Aseron and the Sea of Shattered Stars. 

It is the native language of dragonkind, though often learned as a second language by merchants and scholars, and used as somewhat of a lingua-franca across the east. _rekharhakr_ is considered "colloquial", used for casual literature or messages, but not in governance or religious ceremony. 

The ***khankain*** (royal-born "civilised" dragonkind) of the central plains prefer to use the central plains Yan language [read: Classical Chinese] for state affairs, or Ancient Draconic for solemn and religious matters. The ***arakain*** (wild-born "uncivilised" dragonkind) rarely write, but use draconic as a primary language.

In ancient times the merged sounds *h* and *kh*, *s* and *th* used to have different qualities, though this is now reduced to a tone distinction.

#### 1.2. Pitch Accent

Pitch accent occurs from sound mergers in the language, and are applied per syllable. 

*th* and *kh* are low-tones, syllables with these should be pronounced at the low end of the vocal range.

*s* and *h* are high-tones, syllables with these should be pronounced at normal, or slightly higher than normal vocal range.

Every other syllable is free pitch, pronounced at preference.

***Exception.*** A word-final *s* does not affect the tone of that syllable, as it is usually silent.

#### 1.3. Syllables and Stress

*rekharhakr* syllables are flexible, and usually have a **(C)(C)V(C)(C)** structure. Difficult consonant pairs (e.g. _rk_ and _dn_) are permitted (due to descent from ancient draconic), but usually avoided.

Stress is highly irregular and word-dependent. In romanisation, an áccent over a vowel indicates the stressed syllable. Stress _slightly_ prefers second syllable stress, ignoring most grammatical suffixes. Verb conjugation suffixes can shift stress.

Stress in general can be accent-dependent, and in most cases stress is not an important factor in word legibility.

Long vowels (*aa*, *ii*, etc., or dipthongues *ai*, *ae*) are preferred for stress instead of short vowels. Where this is an exception (e.g. *ásiir* for "sun" vs *asiir* for "Asyr") an áccent is used.

### 2. Orthography 

#### 2.1. Alphabet

The draconic alphabet is called the *akron*, after its first five letters. Each letter is given a name of a word beginning with it. Traditionally, only majuscule script existed, though minuscule "scribe-hand" developed in Dacrame in the west, leading to western draconic having minuscule letters.

This alphabet originated in West Aseron, west of the mountains, and spread eastwards. In the east, it is seen as an informal script which is not held in high regard by dragonkind, merely being seen as useful for communication. Often, the draconic alphabet is used to annotate Yan or other languages' texts as a pronunciation gloss.

#### 2.2. Spelling

Draconic spelling is largely regular--each letter is pronounced. 

The only exception is a final-s, which is dropped to some extent in most accents of all modern dialects.

Romanisation and spelling correspond exactly, with the digraphs *th* and *kh* representing single letters.

> [!comment]
> *othalon* / O Th A L O N.  \[<Drac>OTHALON</Drac>\]    
> *vathas* / V A Th A S.  \[<Drac>VATHAS</Drac>\]

**s-*dropping.*** Final-s sounds are usually silent, being turned into a glottal stop or lengthening the previous syllable slightly.

> [!comment]
> *váthas* / depths.     
> `DEPTHS`. /vasas/ or /vasa/ or /vasa:/

### 3. Grammar Overview

Below is a quick overview of grammar in _rekharhakr_.

***Word order.*** SVO word order + topic comment structure. SOV accepted sometimes in literature.

***Morphology.*** _rekharhakr_ is a mostly **isolating** language with some agglutinative features.

***Marking.*** Dependent-marking: grammatical relationships (e.g. possessives) are marked on the dependent (e.g. the thing doing the posessing)

***Subject alignment.*** Nominative-accusative like English or Chinese.

***Head-initial vs head-final.*** Mostly head-initial like in English or Chinese; **head-final** for adjectives, adverbs, and other descriptors (e.g. titles).

***Dropping.*** _rekharhakr_ is not a pro-drop language, subjects and objects are usually required. Due to influences from the central plains, dropping is acceptable but not mainstream.

***Honorifics.*** _rekharhakr_ has a very basic honorific system that splits the second person pronoun. 

### 4. Noun Morphology

#### 4.1. Nouns

Nouns are simple words. _rekharhakr_ does not possess noun gender/class, noun agreeement, or mark plurality. 

Articles do not exist, and number / count is indicated simply by putting the number word before the noun.

> [!comment]
> _yir rekhar, zor rekhar_ / one dragon, five dragons.     
> `ONE DRAGON, FIVE DRAGON`

Definite emphasis is done with _hir_ "this" or _tak_ "that".

#### 4.2. Pronouns

Pronouns are differentiated by person, gender, and for 1st and 2nd person pronouns by honorific. Pronouns are not differentiated by case.

Person | Pronoun type | Word(s)
-|-|-
1st | singular normal | _zar, za_
·   | singular royal (royal we) | _valkhán_
·   | singular noble | _várkhan_
·   | singular possessive | _záron, ron_
·   | plural (exclusive) | _zárad_
·   | plural (inclusive) | _ánad_
2nd | singular informal | _zii_
·   | singular respectful | _záhar_
3rd | male | _aak_
·   | female singular | _khaa_
·   | female plural | _khaakad_
·   | ungendered singular "they" | _aa_
·   | ungendered plural "they" | _saa_
·   | lower "it" | _thaa_
·   | lower plural "they" | _thiaa_

_-ad_ is the standard suffix for pronoun plurality and is exclusive by default. _ánad_ is used for the inclusive we. This becomes _-kad_ when the pronoun ends with _a_ (e.g. _khaakad_). Third person ungendered pronouns have a different word.

Ordinary nouns may take _-ad_ only in the subject form as emphasis of the grouped nature.

"lower" usually refers to non-dragons, and can apply to both non-sapient things and sapient creatures (e.g. humans, elves, dwarves, etc.). Anything that is of higher "standing" than a dragon (or viewed with sufficient respect) is referred to by standard third person pronouns. 

#### 4.3. Reflexives

Reflexives ("yourself", "myself") are constructed using the word _ilím_ "self" after the noun, e.g. _za ilim_ "myself".

The second person informal reflexive is shortened to _ziim_ "yourself"

#### 4.4. Fused Relatives

The "he, who" or "she, who" construction, or the pronoun "whoever" is a fused relative. In _rekharhakr_, this is expressed by repeating the pronoun.

> [!comment]
> _aak, aak anavín yira_ / He who lights the fire     
> `HE, HE LIGHT FIRE`

#### 4.5. Possession

Possession is marked by the suffix *-on* on the possesser.

> [!comment]
> _rekharon oraes_ / Dragon's zither.     
> `DRAGON-poss ZITHER`

For the first person singular _zar_, the possessive is informally shortened from _záron_ to _ron_.

> [!comment]
> _ron ixiir_ / My love.     
> `I-poss BELOVED`

The second person possessive _zii'on_ is often shortened to _zin_.

Plural endings on pronouns always come before possession.

Possessive suffixes can often shift the stressed syllable back by one, e.g. _xíron_ -> _xirónon_.

#### 4.6. Determiners

_rekharhakr_ has a two level determinative system like English, _hir_ "this" vs _tak_ "that"; _tan_ "here" vs _tak_ "there".

#### 4.7 Attributives

The _-is_ suffix can be added onto nouns to signify that the next noun or phrase belongs to it in context. It is a broad particle that can be thought of as describing location in an abstract way.

For concrete terms, prepositions like _ith_ and _zon_ are preferred. 

> [!comment]
> _xarái'is karínak._ The dancer in the night    
> `NIGHT-attr DANCE-actor`

### 5. Verb Morphology

There are regular and irregular verbs in _rekharhakr_. 

Regular verbs mostly end with _-in_ and conjugate consistently. Rarely, a regular verb will end with a different vowel and _-n_.

Irregular verbs end with _-n_ and conjugate inconsistently.

#### 5.1. Tense

Verbs conjugate by tense.

Tense | Suffix | Example/notes
-|-|-
Infinitive/present | _-in_ | _aak paziin_ / he plays<br/>Present continuous is conjugated separately
Past perfect/imperfect | _ins_ | _aak paziins_ / he played<br/>Add _s_
Past pluperfect | _inir_ | _aak paziinir_ / he had played<br/>Add _ir_
Future | _ix_ | _aak paziix_ / he will play<br/>Replace _-n_ with _-x_
Conditional | _ixa_ | _aak paziixa_ / he would play<br/>Replace _-n_ with _-xa_; also used for talking about the future in past tense
Imperative | _ii_ / - | _pazii nal za_ or _paz nal za_ (informal) / play with me<br/>Replace _in_ or _iin_ with _ii_; informally remove _in_ or replace _iin_ with _ii_

***To be.*** _siin_ (to be) is an example of an irregular verb. Below is the table of its conjugation.

Tense | Form
-|-
Infinitive | _siin_
Present | _siin_
Past | _is_
Pluperfect | _is_
Future | _xii_
Conditional | _xia_
Imperative | _sii_
_Copula_ | _sn_

Irregular verb conjugations are noted in compact form in the [[dictionary]].

***Continuous form.*** Continuous form (e.g. going, playing) is indicated by having the verb _siin_ modally before the main verb. _siin_ and the main verb have to agree tenses.

> [!comment]
> _za siin paziin_ / I am playing    
> _za is paziins_ / I was playing    
> _za xii paziix_ / I will be playing

#### 5.2. Aspect and Modal Particles

Modal particles, such as _kat_ (can), _iir_ (may), _os_ (must), _has_ (shall), _sis_ (want) affect the aspect of a verb. These **don't** conjugate, and go in front of a verb. They are recorded under "MVb" in the [[dictionary]].

> [!comment]
> _za kat paziin_ / I can play     
> `I CAN PLAY`

#### 5.3. Negation

Negation is expressed by the modal particle _da_. This goes before the verb or modal aspect. 

_da_ in isolation is only used for emphasis. Normally, the **prefix** _d-_ is used instead. This is appended directly onto the verb or modal particle, and changes form based on the consonant afterwards:

| original         | transformed | original | transformed |
| ---------------- | ----------- | -------- | ----------- |
| *da*             | *da*        | *dx*     | *dix*       |
| *dk*             | *dkh*       | *dm*     | *dv*        |
| *dn*             | *dan*       | *dl*     | *dr*        |
| *dd*             | *vd*        | *dp*     | *dv*        |
| *dt*             | *dz*        | *dg*     | *dig*       |


> [!comment]
> *za danadéns thaa* / I did not see it
> `I not-SEE-past IT` (note ***dn**adéns* becomes ***dan**adéns*)

>[!comment]
> *za dkhat paziin* / I can't play     
> `I not-CAN PLAY` (note ***dk**at* becomes ***dkh**at*)


#### 5.4. Acting

To refer to the actor of a verb (the "doer"), the infinitive form is marked by the suffix *-ak*.

> [!comment]
> _karínak sa xarái_ / The dancer in the night      
> `DANCE-actor AT NIGHT`

#### 5.5. Passive Voice

Passive voice is constructed using topic-comment structure with copula.

> [!comment]
> _aa triin thúrgar_ / they eat fish     
> `THEY EAT FISH` (active voice)

> [!comment]
> _thúrgar triin sn_ / the fish is eaten      
> `FISH EAT BE-copula` (passive voice)

### 6. Other Word Classes

#### 6.1 Adjectives and Adverbs

_rekharhakr_ makes no difference between adjectives and adverbs. Hence, adverbs are referred to as adjectives exclusively.

_rekharhakr_ is generally _head-initial_, so adjectives are placed after nouns/verbs.

> [!comment]
> _rékhar khrae._ Red dragon    
> `DRAGON RED`

> [!comment]
> _asrai xyae._ Quickly flying    
> `FLY-present FAST`

There is also no significant difference between nouns and adjectives; nouns can be used as adjectival desecriptions. In fact, infinitive form verbs can also be used as adjectival descriptions, if contextually appropriate.

> [!comment]
> _xarai da i'ai._ Starless night    
> `NIGHT NO STAR`

> [!comment]
> _asái yirín._ Burning dawn

Adjectives are formally ordered: qualifier, proper adjective, colour, shape, age, size, quality. 

#### 6.2 Numerals

_rekharhakr_ uses a base-20 counting system, with base 10 used past 100. There _is_ the concept of zero, but it is imported.

3 | _sen_ | 14 | _áexa_ | 50 | _anák'he_
4 | _xirn_ | 15 | _áevas_ | 60 | _senák_
5 | _zor_ | 16 | _áerus_ | 70 | _senák'he_

The suffix _-ka_ is used to represent ordinal numbers, e.g. _senka_ is "third". Note that 
- If the number ends with _-k_, this turns into a glottal stop plus _k_: _nak_ -> _nakka_, is pronounced /naʔk<sup>h</sup>a/
- If the number ends with two consonants, the second one converts into a glottal stop: _ahr_ -> _ahrka_ pronounced /aχ<sup>h</sup>ʔk<sup>h</sup>a/. _xirn_ -> _xirnka_ is similar.
- These are pronunciation differences only, and ordinals are spelled regularly.

#### 6.3 Prepositions

Prepositions (recorded under Pp) are simple particles that are placed before the phrase or object they are preposing. 

These include simple location markers like _ith_ (in), _saa_ (at), _ryin_ (over), comparatives like _san_ (like), as well as qualifiers like _atáne_ (still, yet).

> [!comment]
> _za asrai ryin hakrós tanie._ I fly over great mountains    
> `I FLY-present OVER MOUNTAIN MIGHTY`

> [!comment]
> _arúm san asái yirín._ Eyes like burning dawn    
> `EYE LIKE DAWN BURN`

> [!comment]
> _saa is atáne kath._ They were still of kin    
> `THEY BE-past STILL KIN`

The preposition _sa_ is more complex as it has a broad interpretation generally meaning "in the context of", "of", "about", or "in". It can be used for literary effect, or when a more explicit preposition does not fit.

> [!comment]
> _karínak sa xarái_ The dancer in the night      
> `DANCE-actor AT NIGHT`

#### 6.4 Conjunctions

Conjunctions connect phrases and sentences. They go between phrases, or at the start of sentences, much like English or Chinese.

Common conjugations include _a_ (and) and _ir_ (but). 

> [!comment]  
> _asiir a xai._ the Sun and Moon    
> `SUN AND MOON`

#### 6.5 Question Words

The most fundamental form of the question is by inverting the subject and the acting verb. Optionally, this can be followed by the question particle _na_. 

> [!comment]
> _nadéns zii aak?_ Did you see him?    
> `SEE-past YOU HIM`

For the copula case, there is no verb to use, so the copula phrases are inverted.

> [!comment]
> _rékhar zii na?_ Are you a dragon?    
> `DRAGON YOU question`

An alternate way of asking a yes-no question is by placing the negation particle _da_ after the subject, where the verb originally was.

> [!comment]
> _nadéns zii da aak, na?_ Did you (or did you not) see him?    
> `SEE-past YOU NOT HIM`

Common question words (recorded under Pp) such as _khak_ (what) and _viir_ (how) can be placed at the start of questions.

> [!comment]
> _khak nadéns zii?_ What did you see?   
> `WHAT SEE YOU`

#### 6.6. Interjections

_rekharhakr_ has an extensive, albeit informal set of interjections, mostly meaningless single-syllable phrases such as _akh_, _aa_, _ka_, _kha_, _na_, _ke_. 

Syllables that start with vowels tend to be used at the start of sentences, and those that start with consonants at the end. They have a soft tendency to rhyme with the previous word, and are mostly used for emphatic effect instead of having a set meaning.

### 7. Derivation and Compounding

#### 7.1 Compounding

_rekharhakr_ exhibits noun compounding to a limited degree, usually a noun-descriptor compound. When present, it usually _inverts_ the noun-descriptor structure into descriptor-noun.

> [!comment]
> _idíks rékhar._ The race of dragons    
> `RACE  DRAGON`    
> _rekharidíks._ Dragonkind    
> `DRAGON-RACE = DRAGONKIND`

Such compounds are treated and listed separately in the dictionary. 

Exceptions include some highly compounding descriptors, such as _yelr / yelv-_ (pair) and _tanie / tan-_ (great).

Compounds can always be split into, and are equivalent semantically to the uncompounded forms.

### 8. Phrase Syntax

#### 8.1 Basic Word Order

_rekharhakr_ is a primarily Subject-Verb-Object and Topic-Comment language. However, it features a lot of inverted structures and can be very context-dependent.

Noun phrases go in the order (number) (noun)-(suffixes) (adjectives)

Verb phrases go in the order (modal) (negation)-(verb) (adverbs)

#### 8.2 The Copula

_rekharhakr_ does not have a copula in the standard tense. To say A is B, A and B are put next to each other.

> [!comment]
> _khaa rékhar._ She is a dragon    
> `SHE DRAGON`

The verb _siin_ (to be) _can_ be used as a copula for emphatic effect, in the appropriate tense of the register.

Alternatively, the copula form of _siin_, _sn_ is optional at the end of a topic comment phrase.

> [!comment]
> _khaa siin rékhar._ She IS a dragon.      
> `SHE BE DRAGON`

> [!comment]
> _khaa rékhar sn._ She is a dragon.     
> `SHE DRAGON BE-copula`

#### 8.3 More uses of _siin_

_siin_ in isolation can be used for affirmation ("yes"), as the inverse of _da_ ("no").

The copula form _sn_ can be used at the sentence initial, or after a location predicate, to indicate existence. 

> [!comment]
> _sn sen thúrgar._ There are three fish     
> `BE-copula THREE FISH`

> [!comment]
> _ith damák sn khan rékhar._ There is a dragon king in Damak     
> `IN DAMAK Be-copula KING DRAGON`

Locations _always_ go before the main phrase.

### 9. Information Structure

#### 9.1 Topic, Comment

Topic-comment is the second main sentence structure in _rekharhakr_. It consists of a topic clause, a comment clause, and an optional ending copula (_sn_).

 > [!comment]
 > _damák dai, hákros xanáe sn._ Damak Dai is a divine mountain    
 > `DAMAK DAI, MOUNTAIN DIVINE BE-copula`

#### 9.2 Subject Dropping

_rekharhakr_ is not pro-drop. All subjects and objects are necessary. In very informal speech, subject-dropping is seen, though usually from _khankain_ dragons who have been highly integrated into central plains human culture, and likely treat Draconic as more of a heritage language.

### 10. Register and Discourse

#### 10.1 Honorifics

_rekharhakr_ has a small set of honorifics for direct address. 

First person:
- _zar_ is the standard first person pronoun, and acceptable to use in almost all situations, including with parents, teachers, and seniors.
    - _zarón_ is the possessive first person, the shortened form is _ron_, and only suited for informal situations.
- _siia_ (below) is used as the first person when addressing a ruler as a subject, or someone of rank when you are not. This is not used by _arakain_.
- _várkhan_ (this lord - for landed nobles) and _valkhán_ (this ruler - for the emperor) are restricted and formal first person titles for specific aristocratic ranks.

Second person:

- _zii_ is the informal second person pronoun, acceptable for use with peers or juniors.
- _záhar_ is the formal second person pronoun, used for seniors, such as parents, teachers, or suzerains. 
- In _khankain_ society when speaking to someone of high status, such as a noble or the king, it is customary to use their title (e.g. _arkhán_ (lord), _khan_ (king)) in the subject position.

Third person:

- "lower" pronouns _thaa_ and _thiaa_ ("it") are also applicable to non-dragon sapient races, such as humans, elves, etc.
- This is usually seen as somewhat impolite by _khankain_ society, although often used. Pointed use of standard third person pronouns by a dragon to a non-dragon usually indicates significant relations (friendships, etc.)

#### 10.2 Titles

Titles come after names. 

> [!comment]
> _ástron arkhán_. Lord Astron    
> `ASTRON LORD`    
> _lurkhái khan_. King Lyu [lurkhai]     
> `LURKHAI KING`

#### 10.3 Register

_rekharhakr_ does not necessarily have a highly literary register, as it is used mostly as a colloquial language. 

However, the classical draconic language (_hakr_) is the preferred literary tongue for religious or highly solemn occasions, and for recording ancient magicks.

Fiction written in draconic often borrow from the ancient form for literary effect. The literary register is past tense by default.
